Midiator sits between you and your rig. It turns the presets you've already dialed in into one bank per song on your MIDI controller, with tempo, tap and footswitch layout. It writes straight to a Morningstar or Pirate MIDI controller, and gives you a simple setup sheet for Boss, RJM, JET and M-VAVE.

Scan controller

Already programmed your board? Midiator reads it.

Scan your Morningstar and Midiator finds the pedal presets your own controller presets call up, works out each one's bank and slot, and lets you pick which to add to My presets. It only reads: nothing on the controller changes.

{ How it works }

Three steps to Sunday.

  1. 01

    Catalog

    Add the presets you already use on each pedal. Do it once; add more whenever you save something new.

  2. 02

    Build

    Make a setlist. For each song, name the sections and pick which preset each pedal plays. Set the BPM.

  3. 03

    Send

    Review exactly what will change on the controller, switch by switch, then write. Every write is read back and checked.

{ Safety first }

Never overwrites what you made by hand.

Midiator only writes to a controller preset that's empty or still exactly what Midiator put there last time. Anything you edited yourself is marked and left alone. Nothing gets saved to your pedals, ever.

{ Questions }

Good to know.

Does Midiator change the presets saved on my pedals?

No. It only recalls presets and makes live edits you can hear. It never sends a save command to a pedal. The only thing it writes is your controller's presets (Morningstar or Pirate MIDI), and only the ones it made itself or empty ones.

Do I need to use the AI?

No. Setlists, Songs, My presets and Scan controller are exact MIDI math with no AI involved. Labs, an experimental helper for finding new sounds, is optional and needs your own Anthropic API key.

What do I need to get started?

A Mac, a MIDI controller and your pedals connected to it by MIDI. Midiator writes to a Morningstar or Pirate MIDI controller over USB (turn on MIDI Thru in its settings so Midiator can reach your pedals too). For Boss, RJM, JET and M-VAVE controllers it gives you a setup sheet to copy in.

Can I reuse the same banks every week?

Yes, that's the point. Midiator remembers what it wrote, so next week's setlist can reuse those banks. It updates what changed, clears switches the new song doesn't use, and leaves your own presets alone.

Why does my Mac ask me to confirm the first time I open it?

Midiator isn't registered with Apple yet, so macOS can't vouch for it and asks you to confirm once. Right-click it in Applications and choose Open. After that it opens like any other app, and updates install from inside Midiator.
